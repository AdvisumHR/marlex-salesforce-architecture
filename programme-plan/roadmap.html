Programme roadmapHow the whole thing sequences

Marlex is rebuilding its recruitment platform as its own Salesforce solution, replacing the TargetRecruit product it rents today — then reusing that build for Axxon and new countries, with every external system connected through one integration layer.

In words

The recruitment rebuild is the main sequence, and it is the programme's priority. It starts with foundations — the tooling, governance, glossary, and an inventory of what the current system does, which feeds everything after. Before the build begins, two things clear: a cleanup of the current org (removing dead functionality and technical debt so nothing broken is carried forward), and the design decisions and requirements the build needs to start — naming rules, how components are owned and kept configurable, the data-privacy model, and the business processes each area must follow. The exact build scope is sized by that requirements inventory rather than fixed up front. One thing about the starting point works in the programme's favour: the business already works inside a single standard Salesforce application today, rather than a patchwork of separate apps maintained per team — so the rebuild starts from a simple, single-app footprint with nothing to untangle.

The build then runs. The data model is set as the real foundation, and the CRM, recruitment and back-office areas are built against it. Each area is built, tested and validated with the business as it goes — testing is part of building each capability, not a separate phase bolted on afterwards — and moving the data across from the old TargetRecruit objects to the new ones is part of the rebuild too, rehearsed on a full copy of the data until it runs cleanly. Everything is built to be reusable across the group from the start (driven by configuration, with no hard-coded values) so it can later serve Axxon and other countries without rework.

As the build finishes, the whole solution is reviewed and classified — which parts are group-wide, which are Marlex-specific, which are country-specific. This is where the shared group standard is actually defined, on real proven components, rather than fixed up front. The business then signs the whole thing off in a single acceptance cycle — the data move, the experience and the reports together — so it signs exactly what it will receive. Go-live follows in place, with a short period of close support. At go-live, records keep the access model they have today — the switch moves no privacy boundary; tightening records to private and adding the country dimension is a deliberate, separately-rehearsed stage after go-live.

Two tracks run in parallel with the rebuild and never hold up its go-live. The integration layer (MuleSoft) is built one external system at a time and can be swapped in when each lane is ready. Marketing runs finite campaigns now to earn back its licence and re-points onto the new model after go-live, staying in the same org throughout.

Everything else waits until the rebuild is live. Once it is, the finishing work runs in order: the rented product is uninstalled, the lower-priority and evolutive features held back during the rebuild are built, and then the record-privacy model is tightened. Only after go-live do Axxon's onboarding onto the standard and the rollout to Portugal, Italy and the Philippines become actionable — they all build on the rebuilt platform and the classified standard. The one early exception on the Axxon side is its ERP move to NET4, which is independent of the rebuild and reuses the shared NET4 connection, so it can start sooner.

Three things carry the most risk. Most of the delivery sits with one implementation partner, with no fallback in place today. The matching-and-search engine is bought, not built, so feasibility depends on that engine. And there is a firm date to plan against: the contractual runway on TargetRecruit ends in November 2027 — a renewal-and-renegotiation point with a paid fallback, not a sudden switch-off — so the new solution should be live and TargetRecruit retired with margin before that runway is spent. Whether the switch-over is one big go-live or staged by business area is still open, pending the product team's analysis of how independent the areas really are.

The critical chain

The longest unavoidable path — everything a committed end-date depends on. Each step notes the project it belongs to; almost the whole chain is the recruitment rebuild.

  1. Foundations ATS-RB · the rebuild — Stand up the tooling, governance, glossary, and the inventory of what the current system does that feeds every later phase
  2. Cleanup ATS-RB · the rebuild — Remove dead functionality and technical debt from the current org before the build starts, so nothing broken is carried forward
  3. Design & requirements ATS-RB · the rebuild — Settle the solution-design decisions and gather the requirements the build needs — naming, configurable/ownership rules, the privacy model, and the business processes per area (not a pre-fixed group standard)
  4. Data model ATS-RB · the rebuild — Set the Marlex-owned data model as the real foundation and keep the vendor-translation layer only at the integration edges
  5. Build by area ATS-RB · the rebuild — Build the CRM, recruitment and back-office areas against the data model, in the same org as TargetRecruit — each area designed, built, tested and validated with the business as it goes, and reusable by construction
  6. Classify the standard ATS-RB · the rebuild — Review the finished solution and classify it — group-wide vs Marlex-specific vs country-specific — on real proven components, no production data needed
  7. Data move rehearsed ATS-RB · the rebuild — Build the repeatable pipeline that moves data from the old TargetRecruit objects to the new ones and rehearse it by business area on a full copy of the data — part of the rebuild, not a later phase
  8. Business sign-off ATS-RB · the rebuild — The business signs off the full solution — data move, experience, reports — in a single acceptance cycle
  9. Go-live & support ATS-RB · the rebuild — In-place go-live carrying today's access model forward unchanged, identity switch, and two to four weeks of close support to zero critical incidents
  10. Privatise after go-live SHARE · HR rollout & sharing refactor — Tighten the records that matter to private and add the country dimension — a deliberate post-go-live stage with its own rehearsal and its own roll-back
  11. Group rollout EXP · group expansion — Roll the proven solution out to the rest of the Marlex group — Portugal, Italy, and the Philippines
