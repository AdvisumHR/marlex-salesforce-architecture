Project PORTALThe candidate & recruiter portals

The candidate and recruiter portals — the web front-ends that live today on the platform the programme is retiring. The Salesforce programme owns the connection to them, not the portals themselves; the build sits with an external partner, and its scope, owner and — above all — its timing against the platform switchover are still open.

What this track is

Marlex runs public-facing portals for candidates and recruiters. Today these portals are hosted on the recruitment platform that the rebuild replaces, so they cannot simply carry over untouched — when that platform is retired, whatever it was hosting goes with it. This makes the portals a distinct piece of work with its own owner and its own timing, not a detail inside the rebuild.

The division of labour is settled: the Salesforce programme owns the connection — the integration layer and the Salesforce-side that feed a portal with data and receive what a candidate or recruiter submits. It does not own the portals as products. Building the portals themselves is an external partner's job, on a front-end platform outside this programme. So this page tracks a track the programme depends on but does not itself deliver — which is exactly why its decisions need to be visible in one place.

Two things about the portals are still genuinely open. First, their shape: whether they are Spain-only or serve the wider group, and whether every country gets the same portal or a version tailored to local needs. Second — and this is the decision that drives the plan — when the new portals need to be built and operational relative to the platform switchover. That timing question is set out on its own below, because it is not yet decided and it changes the critical path either way.

When do the new portals have to be live?

The current portals disappear when the old platform is switched off. The new portals, built by the partner on the connection this programme provides, have to appear on the other side. The open question is how those two events line up — and it is a real choice, not a formality, because each option trades continuity against time and risk. This is the decision to close before the plan can commit a portal date.
Built and live before the switchover

The new portals are finished and operational first, then the platform switchover happens with the portals already in place. Candidates and recruiters see no interruption. The cost is that the portal build joins the critical path ahead of go-live: the switchover cannot happen until the partner has delivered, so a slip on the portals becomes a slip on the whole cutover.

Switched over at the same time

The portal switch happens inside the same cutover window as the platform switch — the old portals go dark and the new ones come up together. There is no separate portal go-live to schedule, but the window is tighter and carries more at once, so it needs the partner's delivery and the programme's cutover to be tightly synchronised.

Built and live after the switchover

The platform switches over first and the new portals follow later. This keeps the platform cutover simple and off the portal partner's timeline, but it accepts a gap: for a period after go-live there is no portal, or only a stop-gap, so candidate- and recruiter-facing self-service is interrupted until the partner delivers.

Open — No option is ratified yet, but the choice is leaning. On 2026-07-23 the product side signalled that the portal is not critical on day one and can follow the switchover — a short period without the rebuilt portal is acceptable to the business ('we can wait'), which points toward the built-and-live-after option. Treat that as a lean, not a decision: the portal's owner, the partner's schedule, and whether it serves Spain alone or the wider group are all still open, and the option needs product-owner sign-off to close. The choice still depends on when the partner can deliver, how much portal downtime the business will accept, and how much the programme is willing to add to the cutover's critical path. Once it is made, the roadmap gains a portal milestone and the critical chain may change with it.

Decisions on this track

A running record of what is settled about the portals and what is still open. It grows as each point is decided.

Settled

The programme owns the connection, not the portals

The integration layer and the Salesforce-side that serve the portals are in the programme's scope. The portals themselves are built by an external partner on a front-end platform outside the programme. This boundary is agreed.

The connection is anchored on the shared data model

The contract that carries data to and from a portal is drafted against the programme's shared data model, so it is designed to survive the platform switchover rather than being tied to the platform that is leaving.

Still open

Who owns and funds the portal build

The build sits with an external partner, but the specific owner, the commercial arrangement, and whether it needs a budget or initiative outside the Salesforce programme are not yet confirmed. Until they are, the build has no committed start.

Spain-only or group-wide, standard or tailored

Whether the portals serve only Spain or the wider group, and whether every country shares one portal or gets a tailored version, is still open. This shapes both the build effort and the connection the programme has to provide.

Timing against the platform switchover

The before / same-time / after choice set out above. This is the decision that most affects the plan and is not yet made.

What it connects to

The integration layer

The portal connection is one of the integration lanes — a portal-facing service on the middleware plus a Salesforce-side adapter. The lane builds only the connection; it sits behind the portal shape and the timing decision, because there is no point finishing the pipe before it is known what it feeds and when.

Axxon go-live

Axxon's go-live depends on portals being available. Because the portal build currently has no confirmed owner or date, that dependency rests on something unscheduled — which is the main reason to keep this track visible and its decisions current, so it does not become a surprise blocker at go-live.
